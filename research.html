Tacit Collusion in Pricing Agents: Robustness, Detection, and a Mechanistic Probe of a Pricing LLM

Apart Research AI Collusion Research Sprint · Oct 2026 · team “Code Compels”, with Chewon Cho and Gagana MD

We let AI agents set prices in a simulated market and asked two questions. Can collusion be broken without more oversight? And is a pricing LLM's reaction to a rival real strategic reasoning?

Results and limits

Breaking collusion. A single agent that refuses to follow high prices pulls the market back down. This works for a simple rule-based learner, and more strongly for an LLM pricing on its own.

Inside the model. Steering the model's internal representation moves its own price, so the reaction has a physical trace. But a deviation test showed no punishment: the model cut its price about as much after a rival raised prices as after a rival undercut it.

Limits. The steering direction overlaps with a generic “price” direction, which we report openly. This is an early-stage sprint project, not a peer-reviewed publication.

The SOR Family: A Navigable Continuum of Bellman Training Signals

Sriram Bharadwaj, Raghuram Bharadwaj (IIIT Bangalore), Shalabh Bhatnagar (IISc) · Stochastic Systems Lab, IISc · 2026

Successive Over-Relaxation, applied to the Bellman target, gives a continuum of temporal-difference update rules controlled by one relaxation factor ω. Standard and accelerated TD targets fall out as special cases, and it costs nothing extra to add to a Deep Q-Network.

Results

Up to 65% better performance on Atari environments with Bayesian-optimised ω, and LunarLander-v3 solved in under 160 episodes, with statistical significance checked. The family also carries over to tree search: a TD-SOR variant of AlphaZero applies the relaxed targets to self-play value learning.

My part: co-developed the update rules; built the orchestration that ran parallel hyperparameter sweeps across 6+ GPUs, and the Optuna/SQLite auditing that measures how ω affects gradient stability, sample complexity and seed robustness; implemented the AlphaZero variant in JAX.

UBU: Unmeasurability-Based Unlearning for Safe Reinforcement Learning

Sriram Bharadwaj, Raghuram Bharadwaj · IIIT Bangalore · Oct 2025, revised Aug 2026

Unlearning in RL often hides information rather than erasing it. If an attacker can query a policy on a well-chosen “generating collection” of state–action pairs, Carathéodory's extension theorem says the whole policy is pinned down. We model the attack as a supermartingale over the query history and show reconstruction succeeds almost surely. The defence is to make forbidden states unmeasurable: a sparse mixture-of-experts encoder maps safe states onto a low-dimensional manifold, and forbidden states fall off it.

Results and limits

In a 2D reconstruction attack, the adversary recovers the forbidden region with IoU above 0.95 against a standard value network, and below 0.05 against the defended one. On a 50-dimensional Swiss roll, the encoder cleanly excises a forbidden band.

Limits. Both experiments are controlled proofs of concept. Real encoders are continuous, so the separation is only approximate, and an adversary who learns the manifold geometry is still an open problem.

Circuit Isolation and Control of MuJoCo Agents

Independent · Sep 2026 – present

Find out what causal role the live neurons in a trained agent play, then use that to steer a frozen agent from the outside. The pipeline goes from circuit discovery in the base agent, to a clamp interface, to a goal-conditioned controller. I'm now porting it to a MuJoCo body to see whether “macro” commands like walk, stand or raise a limb can be issued to a fine-motor base policy.

Early results

In the pilot, masking in the coordinate basis beat masking through a sparse autoencoder by a wide margin (a drop of +0.7 against −27.0). The SAE's reconstruction noise dominated the effect.

RL · Reward design · Vision-language models

VLM-RDA: Multimodal Reward Design Agent

Independent · Aug 2026 – present

Text-only reward-design agents like Eureka only see numbers, so they miss reward hacking you can only see. This pipeline feeds evaluation videos straight to a vision-language model, which can spot failures like “zombie dragging” and rewrite the reward code.

How it works

Dual-scale anchoring. The VLM sees the custom reward scale and the true environment scale side by side, so it doesn't drift into reward-hacked optima.

Memory and rollback. A bipartite memory maps visual anomalies to specific reward-code variables, and an elitism rule rolls back to the last stable reward when training diverges.

Backend. A custom PyTorch Conservative Reliable Policy Iteration (CRPI) implementation alongside stable-baselines3 PPO, SAC and TD3, with Optuna sweeps and SLURM deployment.

RL · Network plasticity

Dormant Neurons in RL

Independent · Aug 2026 – present

Tracks post-ReLU units that go permanently quiet in DQN agents and recycles them (ReDo). Zero-Interference Routing (ZIR) then zeroes the weights from recycled neurons into a frozen high-activity sub-network, so repurposing them for a new task can't interfere with the old one. A zero-padding wrapper lets one fixed network move between environments, from CartPole to Acrobot.

Checked with 5-seed ablations and multi-head controls, then scaled up to LunarLander, to separate ZIR's effect from random masking and shared-head interference.

Generative Automotive Design: Structural Disentanglement

Nov – Dec 2025

A domain-specific ControlNet that separates a car's geometry from its paint and texture, so it can be “resprayed” without the model pulling it back toward a generic sedan. Dropping the text prompt 70% of the time forced the model to rely on edge features, producing a “structural locking” effect visible in what I called the Ghost Car phenomenon.

Semantic Layout Control & Mosaic ControlNet

Nov – Dec 2025

Conditioning ControlNet on COCO layouts, with a failure analysis of “concept bleeding” in nested bounding boxes. Mosaic Control is a colour-guidance scheme that abstracts the ground-truth image, keeping spatial correspondence while removing the semantic conflict. Trained on WikiArt within 15 GB of VRAM.

Offline RAG Assistant on Gemini Nano

Kotlin · AICore · SQLite · Sep 2026 – present

A fully offline six-stage retrieval pipeline on Gemini Nano with an on-device Gecko embedder. It fuses dense and BM25 search, adds HyDE and System 2 Attention filtering, and skips generation entirely when retrieval confidence is too low.

Found along the way: a poisoned document could be retrieved as the top chunk and hijack the answer. A pre-filter and prompt hardening blocked all of the injected chunks on-device.

Offline Voice Assistant with llama.cpp and whisper.cpp

C++ · JNI · Vulkan · Sep 2026

Microphone to LLM to speech, fully offline. Custom JNI bridges bring llama.cpp (Qwen2.5-1.5B) and whisper.cpp natively into a Kotlin app, with token streaming across the JNI boundary.

The interesting bug: quantised models gave silently wrong output on the Tensor G5's PowerVR GPU. I traced it to the Vulkan cooperative-matrix and integer-dot-product extensions, and shipped a runtime workaround that restored correct GPU offload.
